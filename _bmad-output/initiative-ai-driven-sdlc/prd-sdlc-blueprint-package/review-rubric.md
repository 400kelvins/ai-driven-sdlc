# PRD Quality Review — AI-Driven SDLC Blueprint Package

Reviewed: `prd-sdlc-blueprint-package.md` + `addendum.md`, checked against `.memlog.md`. Stakes: real product (maintained npx package). User prefers concise PRDs, so findings avoid asking for sections that would only add bulk.

## Overall verdict
The PRD is tight, decision-faithful, and unusually concrete about the review experience: every logged decision in `.memlog.md` made it in, the single persona drives real choices, and the NFRs are product-specific rather than boilerplate. What is at risk is the part the product exists to guarantee: the enforcement chain (FR-17 → FR-21 → FR-22) has no pass bar for "understanding" and no requirement that a recorded "yes" came from a human, the "any repository" promise quietly depends on GitHub Actions and admin-configured branch protection, and the thesis is never stated while the success metrics measure maintainer diligence rather than whether developers actually understand what they approve.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions, not "considerations": "There is no way to approve without understanding" (UJ-1 step 6.6), "Review time is not a goal. Understanding is." (line 46), "the required CI `human-gate` check is the one enforcement point" (NFR-3), "invoke rather than bundle" (FR-3). Open Questions are genuinely open, with owners and deadlines (OQ-1, OQ-2).

What is missing is the cost side. The PRD never names what it gives up. The hardest trade-off in the product, mandatory quiz plus self-rating plus own-words explanation on every change, has no acknowledged friction cost: does a one-line typo fix need the full loop? Someone pushing back with "developers will abandon this after the third quiz" finds no answer. There are no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts at all, even at known tensions such as BMad V7 landing two days after this PRD was written (addendum line 12) or Linux voice support resting on unverified research (addendum line 35).

### Findings
- **medium** Friction trade-off of the understanding loop is unacknowledged (§ UJ-1 step 6, FR-17, FR-18) — every change, regardless of size, goes through the full quiz + confidence + explanation loop, and the PRD never says whether that is intended or what is sacrificed. *Fix:* either state explicitly "the loop applies to every change regardless of size; we accept the friction", or add an FR that scales the review depth to the size of the change (for example, quiz length proportional to the files changed), with a `[NOTE FOR PM]`.
- **medium** No `[NOTE FOR PM]` at real tensions (§ NFR-2, NFR-4) — BMad V7 (alpha, different install command) vs v6.12 pin, and Linux/browser word-boundary support marked unverified in the addendum, are both decisions that will bite during build. *Fix:* add one-line `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts on NFR-4 (which BMad major is pinned for v1) and NFR-2 (Linux path unverified).

## Substance over theater — strong
One persona (Matthew), and he earns his place: the senior-dev-who-won't-read-walls-of-code framing directly justifies the voice, diagram, and quiz FRs. NFRs carry product-specific content (an explicit list of what telemetry may never include, "never overwrites an existing file without asking", "contract tests pass against it"). No innovation theater: the comparables in the addendum ground the gap claim. There is no Vision section, so no Vision theater, but see Strategic coherence for what that absence costs.

### Findings
- **low** F9 Constitution is a single thin FR (§ FR-25) — "holding the gate rules the agent must follow" doesn't say which rules or what happens if the developer edits them to weaken the gates. *Fix:* list the 2–3 rules the starter constitution must contain, and state whether CI enforcement (FR-22) holds regardless of constitution edits.

## Strategic coherence — thin
A thesis exists in the decision log ("redefine AI-driven SDLC; not a huge code blob reviewed at the end; engage the human", memlog line 17) and leaks into the PRD in fragments ("This is the heart of the product", line 23; "Review time is not a goal. Understanding is.", line 46), but the PRD never states it. The document opens straight into UJ-1. A reader who is not the author cannot tell what problem the package solves, for whom, or why existing tools (cc-sdd, OpenSpec, GZKit, per the addendum) fall short. For a maintained product with outside contributors (SM-2), that thesis is what contributors will use to judge which PRs fit.

The features do serve the thesis well. F6–F8 (review, decision, enforcement) are clearly the center of gravity, and the MVP scope kind (experience) matches. The success metrics do not validate the thesis, though. SM-1 (monthly review held) and SM-2 (contribution guide exists, PRs get a response) measure maintainer discipline. Both could hit 100% while every user rubber-stamps quizzes or uninstalls after the demo. CM-1 is just SM-1 negated, not a counter-metric that guards against gaming. This faithfully reflects the user's decision (memlog lines 32–33), so the fix is to add a metric, not replace the existing ones.

### Findings
- **high** Thesis / problem statement absent (§ top of document, before UJ-1) — the core bet ("AI-built code should be approved only by a developer who demonstrably understands it, not by reading the full diff") appears only in the decision log. *Fix:* add a 3–4 sentence "Why" paragraph at the top: problem, who it is for, the bet, and the one-line gap vs comparables. That fits the concise style.
- **high** Success metrics do not test the thesis (§ Success Metrics) — SM-1 and SM-2 measure whether the maintainer is active, not whether gated review produces understanding or trust. *Fix:* keep SM-1/SM-2 and add one thesis metric that fits NFR-1's public-signals-only rule. For example: "SM-3 Gate kept on: of repos that publicly show the `human-gate` check, share still enforcing it 30 days later", or "issues reporting 'approved but didn't understand' / 'bypassed the gate' reviewed monthly, target zero bypass bugs open more than one month". Add a counter-metric for gaming (e.g. quiz pass rate on first attempt near 100% = quiz too easy).
- **low** CM-1 is the inverse of SM-1, not a counter-metric (§ Counter-metrics) — it guards against nothing SM-1 doesn't already catch. *Fix:* replace or supplement it with a counter-metric that catches optimizing the wrong thing (see SM-3 suggestion above).

## Done-ness clarity — thin
Most FRs are crisp and testable: FR-1 (one npx command), FR-4 (one uninstall command), FR-20 (nothing pushed, code stays on disk), FR-23 (advisory comment, cannot merge), FR-24 (agents never merge). The weak spots are exactly the FRs that carry the product's promise.

FR-17 defines understanding as "correct quiz answers, a self-rated confidence, and an own-words explanation that the AI grades", but gives no pass bar for any of the three. What confidence rating blocks a yes? What does the AI grade against, and what score passes? How many questions, and are they generated from the diff? The grader is presumably the same agent that wrote the code, which is a conflict of interest the PRD should at least acknowledge. An engineer could ship FR-17 with a grader that always passes and meet the letter of the requirement.

FR-21/FR-22 are the enforcement chain, and they don't say who can create a "recorded yes". If the gate log is a file in the repo, the coding agent can write it. That would satisfy FR-22 while defeating its purpose. The PRD needs a requirement-level statement that the yes must come from an action the agent cannot perform. The mechanism (human GitHub approval, signed commit, interactive TTY confirmation) can go to architecture. "Exact code" also needs a rule for rebases and merge-from-main: does any change after yes invalidate it?

FR-13 promises a function-level call diagram for "any existing git repository, whatever its stack" (FR-1). Function-level call graphs require per-language analysis. Without a supported-language list or a fallback (module-level diagram), this FR has no finish line.

### Findings
- **high** Understanding pass bar undefined (§ FR-17, FR-18) — no threshold for the confidence rating, no grading criteria for the own-words explanation, no quiz size or source, and the grader is the implementing agent. *Fix:* add testable consequences, for example: "Yes is enabled only when all quiz questions are answered correctly, confidence ≥ 3 of 5, and the explanation mentions every changed module named in the call diagram; a confidence below 3 re-shows the diagram." State whether the grader is a separate prompt/agent from the implementer.
- **high** A recorded "yes" can be forged by the agent (§ FR-21, FR-22, NFR-3) — nothing requires the gate-log entry to originate from a human action, so the single enforcement point can be satisfied by the very agent it gates. *Fix:* add to FR-22: "A yes counts only if it is bound to an action the coding agent cannot perform on its own (e.g. made by the human's GitHub identity); CI rejects gate-log entries without that proof." Also state that any code change after the yes (including rebase) invalidates it.
- **medium** Call diagram has no finish line for "any stack" (§ FR-13 vs FR-1) — function-level call graphs need per-language support. *Fix:* name the v1 languages for function-level diagrams and add "other stacks get a file/module-level diagram", or make the diagram module-level everywhere (UJ-1 step 6 already says "module diagram").
- **medium** Repository init output unspecified (§ FR-8) — "records its stack, structure, and conventions as context" has no artifact or check. *Fix:* name the output (e.g. a generated AGENTS.md block plus a project-context file) and what it must contain, so it is testable.
- **medium** Voice input has no NFR (§ FR-9) — "by voice or by typing" introduces speech-to-text, but NFR-2 covers only spoken output. *Fix:* either drop "by voice" for v1 or add speech-to-text platform coverage to NFR-2.
- **medium** Voice-only review lacks a text path (§ FR-12, NFR-2) — no requirement for a transcript or captions for muted environments or hearing-impaired developers, and the Linux path rests on research the addendum marks unverified. *Fix:* add "the spoken summary is always also shown as text with word highlighting", and tag the Linux/browser fallback `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **low** Decision-handling FRs leave the PR author implicit (§ FR-19, FR-24) — "moves on to a pull request" doesn't say who opens it, and "agents never merge" doesn't say how that is enforced (branch protection? token scope?). *Fix:* one clause each: "the agent opens the PR"; "the installer recommends branch protection that requires the `human-gate` check".

## Scope honesty — thin
Non-Goals do real work (team roles, deploy/retro, telemetry) and match the decision log. But for a real-product PRD, open items are under-declared rather than over-declared: one inline `[ASSUMPTION]` (line 39), no Assumptions Index, zero `[NOTE FOR PM]`.

The biggest silent assumption is GitHub. FR-1 promises "any existing git repository", but FR-10 (GitHub Issues), FR-19 (pull request), FR-22 (required CI check), and FR-23 (PR comment) all assume GitHub, GitHub Actions, and a repo admin willing to mark `human-gate` as required in branch protection, which the installer usually cannot do without admin rights. A GitLab or Bitbucket user installs successfully and gets gates with no enforcement, and nothing tells them that. The prerequisites are also silent: the addendum says BMad needs Node 20.12+, Python 3.10+, and uv, so "whatever its stack" is true of the target repo but not of the developer's machine.

Other silent omissions: agents beyond Claude Code and Codex (the addendum lists ~40 that spec-kit supports; are Cursor/Gemini users turned away, or do they get a generic setup?), and what NFR-5's "leaves the repository as it was" means for things outside the working tree (GitHub issues, PRs, branch-protection settings, the demo branch).

### Findings
- **high** "Any repository" silently means "GitHub with admin rights" (§ FR-1 vs FR-10, FR-19, FR-22, FR-23) — enforcement, tracker, and PR flow all require GitHub/Actions and branch-protection setup, and machine prerequisites (Node, Python, uv) are unstated. *Fix:* add a short "Requirements" line: "v1 requires a GitHub-hosted repo with Actions; a repo admin must mark `human-gate` as required; developer machine needs Node ≥20.12, Python ≥3.10, uv." Add a Non-Goal: "Non-GitHub hosts (GitLab, Bitbucket) get local gates but no CI enforcement in v1."
- **medium** Unsupported agents not declared (§ NFR-3, Non-Goals) — Codex and Claude Code are supported, but there is no statement about the other agents spec-kit supports. *Fix:* add a Non-Goal: "Agents other than Claude Code and Codex are unsupported in v1; the installer exits with a clear message."
- **medium** BMad version pin left open (§ NFR-4, FR-3) — v6.12 and V7 install differently (addendum lines 11–12), and V7 alpha lands this week. *Fix:* state which major v1 pins, or add OQ-3 with owner and deadline.
- **medium** Uninstall scope ambiguous (§ NFR-5, FR-4) — "as it was before install" can't cover issues, PRs, gate-log history, or branch-protection rules already created. *Fix:* scope it: "Uninstall removes every file the installer added and restores files it changed; it does not delete GitHub issues, PRs, or gate-log history."
- **medium** No Assumptions Index; few inferences tagged (§ whole document) — only one `[ASSUMPTION]`, and inferences such as "the human's GitHub account is the reviewer identity" and "Linux voice works via browser" aren't tagged. *Fix:* tag them inline and add a 3–5 line Assumptions Index at the end.

## Downstream usability — adequate
IDs are contiguous and unique (UJ-1–2, FR-1–25, NFR-1–5, SM-1–2, CM-1, OQ-1–2), and FR↔NFR cross-references resolve (FR-3→NFR-4, FR-4→NFR-5, FR-12→NFR-2, FR-22→NFR-3). Feature groups make epic slicing obvious. This PRD will feed architecture and stories (and spec-kit), so the gaps matter.

There is no Glossary, and the load-bearing nouns ("gate", "story", "gate log", "fingerprint", "understanding", "review") are used without definition. "Gate" is the most overloaded: FR-11 names a plan gate and an implement gate, and F6/UJ-1 describe "the review". Is the review the implement gate, or a third gate? UJ-1 also skips the plan gate entirely (step 4 tracker → step 5 implement), so the journey and FR-11 disagree about how many times Matthew says yes or no. FRs don't trace back to UJ steps, but the parallel structure (UJ-1 step 6 ↔ F6) makes that mostly recoverable.

### Findings
- **medium** Gate count ambiguous between UJ-1 and FR-11 (§ UJ-1 steps 5–6, FR-11, F6) — the FRs imply plan gate + implement gate (+ review?), while UJ-1 shows a single yes/no at review. *Fix:* in FR-11 state "the implement gate is the review in F6", and add a plan-gate step to UJ-1 between steps 4 and 5 (or state that the plan gate is lighter, e.g. no quiz).
- **medium** No Glossary for load-bearing terms (§ whole document) — "gate", "gate log", "fingerprint", "story", "understanding" will be interpreted differently by architecture and story writers. *Fix:* add a 5–6 entry glossary (one line each); it fits the concise style.

## Shape fit — strong
A developer tool whose value is an interaction experience: UJs with a named protagonist are load-bearing here, and two journeys (first-run and steady-state) is the right number. The capability-spec style FRs underneath fit a CLI package. Brownfield is the target context (installing into existing repos), and the PRD handles it at the requirement level (NFR-5, FR-8). The only shape gap is the missing "Why" (covered under Strategic coherence) and a requirements/prerequisites line (covered under Scope honesty), both expected for a published, maintained package.
